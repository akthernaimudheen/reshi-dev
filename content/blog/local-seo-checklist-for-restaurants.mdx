Most restaurant owners we meet have been told to "do SEO" by someone who never explained what that means for a business whose customers are searching from a car three kilometres away. Local search is a different game from national search, and the things that matter are more boring and more fixable than most agencies let on.

This is the list we work through, in order. The order matters — the later items barely move until the earlier ones are clean.

## Fix the profile before the website

Your Google Business Profile drives more local traffic than your website does. That is uncomfortable if you just paid for a website, but it is true, and it is also good news because the profile is free.

### Categories

Pick the most specific primary category that describes you. "Seafood restaurant" beats "Restaurant" every time. Add secondary categories only where they are genuinely accurate — stuffing them dilutes relevance rather than broadening it.

### Hours, including the exceptions

Wrong hours are the single most common reason for a bad first experience. Set holiday hours in advance. If you close between lunch and dinner, say so.

### Photos, dated

Profiles with recent photos get meaningfully more engagement. Upload a handful every month rather than forty once a year.

## Then fix what search engines see

### Page speed on a real phone

Test on a mid-range Android device on mobile data, not on your laptop. If the page takes more than three seconds to become useful, nothing further on this list will save you.

### A menu that is text, not a PDF

A PDF menu is invisible to search and painful on a phone. Real HTML, with dish names as text, is one of the highest-return changes a restaurant can make.

### Structured data

Mark up the restaurant, the menu and your reviews. This is what produces the rich results that make your listing take up more space than your competitor's.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Restaurant",
  "name": "SeaKing Restaurant",
  "servesCuisine": "Seafood",
  "priceRange": "₹₹",
  "acceptsReservations": "True"
}
```

### One page per thing people search for

People do not search for "our story". They search for "seafood near me", "best fish curry in Kochi", "restaurants open now". Build pages that answer those searches honestly. Do not spin out fifty near-identical location pages — Google has been good at spotting that for years.

## Then work on the signals you do not control

### Citations

Your name, address and phone number should be identical everywhere they appear. Inconsistency here is a slow leak rather than a catastrophe, but it is worth an afternoon.

### Reviews, requested properly

Ask after a good experience, in person or by message, with a direct link. Never incentivise. Never bulk-request. Respond to every review, especially the mediocre ones — a thoughtful reply to a three-star review does more for a prospective diner than ten five-star ratings.

### Links from local sources

A mention in a local food blog or a neighbourhood association page is worth more than a hundred directory submissions. This is slow work and there is no shortcut.

## What to expect

If your foundation is genuinely broken, the first four items typically show movement within six to eight weeks. Ranking in the map pack for competitive terms takes longer — three to six months is realistic, and anyone promising faster is either lucky or lying.

The honest summary: local SEO is not clever. It is a list of unglamorous things done properly and then maintained. Most of your competitors will not maintain them, which is the entire opportunity.
